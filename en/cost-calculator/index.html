Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is uploaded or saved. The default example is a batch of butter cookies. Its prices are placeholders, so replace them with what you actually pay. Any currency works: the result is in whatever you type.

How it works

Row cost = grams used ÷ pack weight × pack price

  • Batch ingredient cost adds up every row. A row with no pack price or pack weight counts as 0.
  • Per piece = batch ingredient cost ÷ pieces in the batch.
  • Per 100 g = batch ingredient cost ÷ total ingredient weight × 100. This uses the weight before baking; bakes lose water in the oven, so the cost per 100 g of the finished bake is somewhat higher.
  • Reference price = cost per piece ÷ cost share. If a cookie costs 0.50 in ingredients and you want ingredients to be 30% of the price, the reference price is 0.50 ÷ 30% ≈ 1.67.

What it leaves out

This counts ingredients only. Packaging, energy, equipment, platform or market fees, delivery and your own time are not included, so the result is an estimated ingredient cost, not your full cost and not your profit. Add those separately when you set a price. There is no single right cost share; it depends on what you bake and where you sell.

Three places the numbers go wrong

  • Keep everything in grams. Enter eggs by their weight without the shell. For milk or cream sold by volume, use the net weight on the pack or weigh it once.
  • Pack price means the whole pack, not a unit price. A 1,000 g bag of flour that costs 2.00 goes in as 2.00 and 1000.
  • Update when prices move. Butter, cream and chocolate change often; re-enter what you last paid.
