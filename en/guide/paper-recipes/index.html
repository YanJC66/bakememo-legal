How to turn paper recipes into digital ones

Handwritten notebooks, cookbook pages, saved web posts — scattered recipes are hard to compare or improve. Getting them into one consistent digital format is the first step.

Three ways to do it

MethodBest forWatch out for
Type it inShort or hard-to-read handwritten recipesSlowest, but least error-prone
Photo text recognitionBooks and printoutsAlways check every number afterwards
Paste textRecipes from websites and messagesStrip ads and unrelated paragraphs first

Be careful converting units

Many recipes use cups and spoons, and a cup of flour, sugar or liquid weighs very different amounts. Convert each ingredient separately; the most reliable approach is to weigh it the next time you bake and record the actual grams.

Check every line and fill the gaps

Text recognition and copy-paste can turn “150” into “15” or merge two ingredient lines. Proofread line by line against the original, then add what the original left vague: oven temperature, time, tin size and the state to look for at each stage.
