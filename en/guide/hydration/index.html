How to calculate dough hydration

Hydration is water as a percentage of flour. If you bake with a sourdough starter, the flour and water inside the starter count too — leave them out and the number reads low.

The basic formula

Hydration = water weight ÷ flour weight × 100%

It’s simply the baker’s percentage of water. 500 g flour with 350 g water is 70% hydration. If you use more than one flour, add them together as 100%.

With a starter: split it into flour and water

A starter is flour plus water. At 100% starter hydration, 100 g of starter is 50 g flour and 50 g water.

Flour in starter = starter weight ÷ (1 + starter hydration)
Water in starter = starter weight − flour in starter

Example: 500 g flour + 350 g water + 100 g starter.

Same formula: 70% if you only count added water, 72.7% with the starter. When you compare with someone else’s recipe, or wonder why a dough feels wetter than expected, check which number each of you means.

The basic white sourdough is 450 g bread flour, 324 g water, 90 g starter and 9 g salt. Counting the starter (100% hydration): 495 g flour, 369 g water — about 74.5% total hydration.

Changing hydration: how much at a time

  • Change only hydration. The example in BakeMemo’s Baking basics: bake the base formula, then raise only hydration by 3% next time, compare, and decide whether to keep it.
  • Too wet, sticky and hard to shape: when hydration is already 75% or more, BakeMemo’s after-bake check says “possibly too much water” and suggests lowering hydration by 3–5% and changing nothing else. The trade-off: the crumb may be a bit tighter.
  • Sticky at a moderate hydration: gluten may need more strength. Do a few more sets of folds and work with wet hands rather than adding flour.

Write down this bake’s hydration, dough temperature and result, so the next bake shows what those 3% did. See how to log a bake.
