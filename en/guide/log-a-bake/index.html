How to log a bake so you can review it

A failed loaf isn’t the problem — not knowing what was different is. Log every bake the same way and the next one has something to compare against.

What a bake log should include

ItemWhy it matters
Grams and baker’s percentagesPercentages make doughs of different sizes directly comparable
Flour brand and typeFlours absorb water very differently; the same hydration can feel completely different
Room and dough temperatureTemperature drives fermentation speed and explains results better than time alone
Time and state at each stageTime isn’t enough — note states like “about 1.5× volume, bubbles on the surface”
Oven temperature, time and steamColour, crust and oven spring often come down to this step
Rating, photo and crumb shotRate crumb, taste and looks; trends only show up after a few bakes
One thing to change next timeWrite the plan while the bake is still fresh in your mind

Change one variable at a time

If one bake raised hydration, extended the cold retard and switched flour, a better result can’t tell you which change helped. Change one thing per bake and write it at the top of the log.

Read bakes of the same recipe side by side

A single log is of limited use; the value is in comparison. First bake at 68% hydration, second at 73%, third with a longer retard — and the rating for each. Lined up in order, the link between changes and results becomes clear.

Differences between a few bakes are clues, not proof: weather, flour batches and technique all vary. When you see a clear trend, repeat a bake or two to confirm it.
